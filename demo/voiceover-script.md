# Mr. Tatkal — pitch video voiceover script (~2 min)

Timings are guides; match them to the final cut of `demo/final-demo.mp4`.

**[0:00 – 0:15] Hook (title card, then the live IRCTC page)**
"Every Indian knows the panic: 11:00 sharp, Tatkal opens, and the seat is gone in seconds. We asked a different question — what if an agent trained for that moment, using a computer exactly like a person does?"

**[0:15 – 0:35] The idea (architecture view in the control room)**
"Meet Mr. Tatkal. One general-purpose harness: hands for the shell, files, browser and desktop; eyes through screenshots and page structure; and a loop that plans, acts, observes and verifies. New jobs are not new code — they are skill files."

**[0:35 – 1:00] Login and the human handoff (CAPTCHA/OTP)**
"Watch it open IRCTC and load its login skill. Credentials never touch the model — they go in from a secure vault. When it hits a CAPTCHA and OTP, it doesn't guess. It pauses, asks the person through its own handoff channel, and resumes the moment the human says done."

**[1:00 – 1:30] Search, choose, fill**
"Roorkee to Haridwar, tomorrow. It reads the results, compares fares, picks the cheapest available option — the cheapest Tatkal ticket when the window is open — and fills the passenger details from a local profile. Every step is logged with a screenshot and the reason for the action, so judges can replay the whole run."

**[1:30 – 1:50] Fare review and payment**
"At the fare review it stops and asks for approval. Payment is the one thing the human does. The agent never pays for you."

**[1:50 – 2:10] Skills that learn, and close**
"After the run, the agent writes what it learned back into the skill, so the next booking is faster. Same harness, any task. Mr. Tatkal — train the agent once, catch the train every time."

## Recording notes
- Show the control room (agent steps + screenshots + handoff prompt) beside the browser.
- Do not show the password, OTP, UPI id or payment screen in the final cut; blur or cut those frames.
